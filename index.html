SHRUTI SYSTEM (22 Microtones)

Shruti = the smallest perceptible interval of pitch. The octave (Sthayi) is divided into 22 shrutis, from which 12 swarasthanas and 7 swaras are derived.

22 SHRUTIS in 1 OCTAVE
Sa4 shrutis
Ri3 shrutis
Ga2 shrutis
Ma4 shrutis
Pa4 shrutis
Dha3 shrutis
Ni2 shrutis

Total = 4+3+2+4+4+3+2 = 22 shrutis per octave

SWARA SYSTEM (7 Swaras → 16 Swarasthanas)

7 base swaras (Sapta Swara): Sa Ri Ga Ma Pa Dha Ni. Sa & Pa are fixed (Prakriti). The remaining 5 (Vikriti) take multiple positions producing 16 swarasthanas (12 distinct pitches).

Ga (Gandhara)3G1, G2, G3Vikriti
Ma (Madhyama)2M1, M2Vikriti

Dha (Dhaivata)3D1, D2, D3Vikriti
Ni (Nishada)3N1, N2, N3Vikriti
Total swarasthanas = 1 + 3 + 3 + 2 + 1 + 3 + 3 = 16 (12 unique pitches; R2=G1, R3=G2, D2=N1, D3=N2)

Audava / Shadava / Sampurna Classification

Sampurna

All 7 swaras present in Aroh & Avaroh (e.g., Sankarabharanam).

Shadava

6 swaras (one omitted), e.g., Sriranjani.

Audava

5 swaras (two omitted), e.g., Mohanam, Hindolam.

Vakra

Non-linear / zigzag movement, e.g., Anandabhairavi.

CARNATIC TALA SYSTEM (5 JATHIS × 7 TALAS = 35 TALAS)

Tala = the cyclic time framework. Sapta Tala (7 talas) combined with 5 Jathis (laghu counts) produces the 35-Tala scheme. Each tala has Anga units: Laghu (l), Drutam (O), Anudrutam (U).

Anga (Building Blocks of Tala)

Laghu (l)

Variable beats (3, 4, 5, 7, 9) per the chosen Jathi. Counted: clap + finger taps.

Drutam (O)

Always 2 beats: clap + wave.

Anudrutam (U)

Always 1 beat: clap only.

5 Jathis (Laghu Counts)

Tisra3
Chatusra4
Khanda5
Misra7
Sankirna9

GAMAKA (Ornamentation) — Dasa Vidha (10 types)

Gamaka = the soul of a Raga — graceful oscillations and slides between swaras. The classical text lists 15 Gamakas; the practical "Dasa Vidha" (10) is most common.

1. Aarohana

Smooth ascent through swaras.

2. Avarohana

Smooth descent through swaras.

3. Dhalu

Sudden glide from a higher to lower swara.

4. Sphurita

Two adjacent swaras quickly with stress on the latter.

5. Kampita

Vigorous oscillation — most characteristic of Carnatic.

6. Aahata

Striking from below to a higher swara.

7. Pratyahata

Striking from above to a lower swara.

8. Tripuccha

Three-fold quick succession on a swara.

9. Andolita

Slow, swing-like oscillation.

10. Murchana

Sequential ascent/descent across an octave.

COMPOSITION FORMS — KRITI / KIRTANA Structure

A Carnatic Kriti (also Kirtana / Keertana) typically has three parts. Compositions also include Sangati (variations) and Chittaswaras.

KRITI / KIRTANA
Pallaviopening refrain · main theme (1–2 lines)
Anupallavisecond section · usually higher octave
Charanamconcluding verse(s) · longer, devotional content
Sangatimelodic variations of a line
Chittaswaramfixed solfa passage
Madhyama Kaladouble-speed passage
Mudracomposer's signature

Other Major Compositional Forms

Geetham

Simple beginner composition; single section.

Swarajati

Stepping stone between Geetham and Varnam.

Varnam

Compact piece showcasing the entire Raga; opening item of concert.

Kriti / Kirtana

Main devotional composition (Pallavi + Anupallavi + Charanam).

Padam / Javali

Slow, expressive love-themed lyrical pieces.

Tillana

Rhythmic, dance-oriented piece using jathis (sollukattu).

Ragamalika

"Garland of ragas" — composition moves through several ragas.

Viruttam / Slokam

Free-rhythm, prose-like devotional verse.

MANODHARMA — IMPROVISATIONAL PERFORMANCE STRUCTURE

Manodharma Sangeetam = creative / improvised music. The 5 main expressions:

MANODHARMA SANGEETAM
1. Raga Alapanafree-rhythm exploration of the raga (no tala)
2. Tanampulsed rhythmic improvisation, syllables "ta-nam"
3. Niravalmelodic variations on a chosen lyric line within tala
4. Kalpanaswaramimprovised swara passages culminating on a Kriti line
5. Pallavi (RTP)composed line developed through Niraval & Kalpanaswaram

RTP — Ragam · Tanam · Pallavi (centerpiece of a concert)

Ragam (Alapana)

Slow, free-flow exploration to establish the raga's character.

Tanam

Rhythmic, pulsating phrases without fixed tala — bridges Alapana & Pallavi.

Pallavi

One composed line set in a chosen tala, taken through:

  • Niraval (lyric variations)
  • Trikalam (3 speeds)
  • Kalpanaswaram (improv swaras)
  • Often Ragamalika swaras at the end
